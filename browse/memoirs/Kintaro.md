## The Boy, the Bear, and the Brightest Flower

### The Wind Howls, and the Trees Fall Silent

My Yama-uba, old ogress of the wild. I speak to you in my thoughts because here no one hears me.

You raised me like the old mountain Ashigara shelters the forest. You watched me from above among the birds and trees, where the wilderness was free and untamed like me! The animals were my brothers and sisters. I raced deer, laughed with monkeys in the river’s flow. And Kuma taught me to wrestle bears whose shadows swallowed his own. We made the ground tremble with our laughs when I rode him.
I bent trees into bridges, so the villagers could cross the feral river. I made fallen limbs mine to chase away the angry things that had forgotten their names. They fought me with fierce eyes, and I answered with fiercer strength! 

I feared nothing, for I never fought alone. The mountain was with me. And I would forever shelter the mountain, as the mountain had sheltered me!

I can still hear the howling wind from the West, the stillness that followed and the silence swallowing the birds’ song. Clouds darkened the sky and the mountain's shadows stopped dancing across the plains. 

You entrusted us with a message enclosed in a flower and sent us away. 
So I bear Ashigara’s hope, I remember your face and long for my return.

### A Glowing Firefly Is a Lonely Light

My Yama-Uba. I have seen forests at night. I have met villagers who lost everything. And I have fought fierce beasts who knew no laws!
If you would see what I am seeing here, would you even believe your eyes? 

I did not know the sun could only pierce through Ashigara’s forest canopy. I thought everywhere, monsters and spirits only waited to be healed! 
Here, there is no light to cast  shadows, and the only shadows I know are those resting in my fellow fighters' eyes.
With Kuma at my side, I joined their strange fellowship: a winged onryō afraid of the sun. A maiden made a wolf by night. An old man forced to create yōkai creatures to defend himself.

They say that all things here are wounded and broken. That they are all victims of some dark curse… 

Kuma and I are not!

We follow them, but we do not flicker. Among fireflies humming in rhythm, two steady lights have nothing to say.

We are visitors in this land that does not know your watchful eyes, and we will soon be back home.

### The Brightest Flower Grows in the Shade

Yama-Uba. I remember the day you picked the little golden flower! The mountain and the wind were playing their game: the malicious wind pushed the clouds' shadows across the hillside grass, but the mountain's shadow swallowed them whole. Kuma and I wrestled. You were picking flowers. 
Some you gently plucked from their stem, others you ignored. It only took you one look!
In the shades of the cliffside, you picked the flower who stole the sun’s color. Tightly closed, not yet bloomed. You gently put it in your satchel and refused to tell me if this one was to harm or heal. You only said: 

           “Too much sun will fade the yellow out of it.”

Then the wind howled from the West, and you gave me the flower. It was still sleeping, tight in its petals:

            “Go to the West, give this yamabuki flower to my sister when the time comes, but do not open it until the moment of true need”.

Old ogress of the wild, I know your mischievous eye! And I know better than to ask why the old mountain challenges the wind.

### A Sleeping Bear Is More Dangerous than a Woken One

I hid the yamabuki flower in Kuma’s fur! 

If someone tries to reach for it, well… 
They do not know Kuma only hates one thing: it is to be woken up in the middle of his nap. 
They do not know Kuma can make a forest tremble when his stomach is empty. 
And they do not know Kuma’s favorite thing is to wrestle those who try to approach him. 

Kuma takes a lot of naps. Food is hard to find in these empty lands. But my companions are wise enough not to try and pet the bear! I wish someone tried to take the yamabuki flower. I would enjoy watching Kuma wake up!

Yama-Uba, the golden bud is safe and closed tight. I do not even smell it. I will make sure it is untouched until time comes! 

We endure the bare hills and howling islands of this Westland, but we have not found your estranged sister. Yet! Sometimes, I reach for the golden flower inside Kuma’s fur, just to see how yellow it shines! Its bright petals remind me of the mountain’s sunrises.

### A Wriggling Snake Is Slower than the Rising Sun

Yama-Uba. I fight for a reason none of them share. They swore to defeat Baba Yaga. I was sent to bring her a flower.

Do you remember the spirit who took the shape of a giant snake to strangle you while you slept? The yōkai sneaked through the high grass when I jumped in front of it and chopped it to pieces before it could wriggle! 

I do not know how many times the sun rose and set. And finding your strange sister is not fast! The route is filled with stranger kaiju. Kuma and I enjoy fighting these beasts who lost their minds, or maybe they only share one and do not know they are what they are. 
They are not Ashigara's monsters. But I fight them with the same strength, uprooting the rare trees to throw their way, and splitting their mindless heads with my broadaxe!
Here, there are no brothers, no sisters,  to hear my call. Only Kuma, and the little drakeling that follows the big man who forgot how to smile. 

Then I thought! You might have hidden a clue inside the flower. So I sneaked a peek inside Kuma’s fur and saw the yamabuki flower had bloomed! 
I carefully looked inside, and only with my eyes like you taught me! 
Empty.

### A Flower that Blooms Is Never Empty

Empty. No message to be found. Did I lose it? Impossible! Why would you send me here with an empty flower, Yama-Uba? 

Just a yamabuki flower. Fully bloomed. The color of the sun absorbed. The only golden bud to see here. All around us is rotten and bare, and twisted. 

I think I understand now, old ogress of the wild. This tiny flower shines like the sun bouncing on the river in Ashigara. I know that river. I know the giant carp who swims against its current, trying to ascend the waterfall and transform. I rode that fish. How many times did we wrestle beneath the falls, the mighty koi and I? The carp always tries. I always answer.

And I will always be Kintarō, the golden boy of mighty strength! Child of Ashigara who was never meant to carry a message. 

I am the message. I am the yamabuki flower who will always bloom. 
I am the sun who will always rise, no matter the clouds.

### The Golden Child Knows More than the Old Witch

Old mother of the wild, we defeated your twisted sister. The others can rest and heal. Some talk about what they lost and gained in the battle. 

I am unchanged. And I want nothing from the witch who stood before me.

I gave her the yamabuki flower. Open and bright! At first, she laughed. 
I just asked her: 

“Do you hear it, old aunt of the shadows? 
The wind in the trees? 
The laughter of the monkeys in the river? 
The breath of the bear? Do you see it, crooked ogress of the West? 
The glowing dance of the fireflies?
And the sun rising over the plains?”

I just reminded her. All those things that do not change. The shadows can hide them, but only for a moment. The sun always pierces through the clouds! 

The only thing that changed, my Yama-Uba, is that now, I know.
