## The Curse of Merlin Farseer

### A plea to Vivian, my captor and my cage

I know these words were written by my hand, 
some centuries past before thee, Vivian, 
did trap me in a tomb of amber stone.
I know within them hides a glimpse of morrows
one day proven true. My prophecies
are legend. Oracle and sage, the mystic 
mage, Merlin's verse the voice of Delphi.

I know as well for I foresaw its coming
how you trapped me in a jail cell staved 
with tree-root bars. Thou'st needed sunshine free 
of mentor's shadow. Hence I let thee cage
me, mind and body, 'til thee found thy footing
free to walk amongst the trees alone.
Alas, I do not know, nor can I fathom
why thee let me leave my prison cave
bereft of future sight. I read my words
once written with full grasp of deeds
unfolding under moons to come, yet am
a fool before their meaning now. I ask,
dear Vivian, what are thy plans for me?

Thine mentor past and lover still, a trusting 
friend unto the Otherworld beyond.

### Prophecies are curses garbed in brighter clothes

This verse I wrote four centuries past and had
forgotten every word. 'Til Morgan Fey 
unrolled the scroll and asked me what would follow.
For Arthur's bones now lack his skull and Mordred's 
ghost has turned into a crown thief, stalking 
tombstone hollows. Damned I'd be, were I
to know, my forsooth inklings dried and withered.
The Fey Queen Morgan would not hear my plight. 
She'd known me enigmatic, every word 
a riddle, every phrase a coaxing spell.
We too had once been lovers for a century,
maybe, two. Once learning all I'd had 
to teach, she sprouted wings, away she flew.
And now though I had carefully explained
how Vivian had locked me up and freed 
me leaving mind and soul intact, without 
my power to oracle, nor recall futures seen.

Fey Morgan would not hear of it. She thinks
me old and vain. "While Reverie is dying
Merlin laughs and has his game, 'til demon 
mischief's course is run and all our joys 
unsung." Dear Vivian return to me, I cannot
help without my power of prophecy.

### How songs rewritten curse this wretched age

My prophecie when read without my soothing 
sight, give pause to ponder whether weed 
and mead were not at work when put to quill.
Alas the Sandman, Lord of Sleep, I came 
to meet in seeking answers to my blindness.
"Merlin mage, you've wandered far astray.
The only remedy for those who wish 
to see the world that is to be, is planted
deep within your fabled family tree."
And then he proffered me a hero's quest.
"Embrace the Raven's Watch old friend as wizard,
sage and leader. Free our lands of witch's 
minions and your vision shall return."

And so my Vivian off I go to fight
against these foreign demons, well aware 
that my own father born of hellish blood
might run amongst their ilk and fiendish kind.
If this quest should lead to where you keep
my powers locked, I do no longer know.
This journey is a first for me to witness
only that which stands before me in
the now. And so to serendipity 
I vow to take this path where'er it leads.
Your erstwhile wizard sage of Raven's Watch

### Alas, Cassandra's prophecies were given no import

Again 'tis I who wrote that cryptic verse
some centuries ago. And only now 
is meaning met. Cassandra comes to mind.
In Troy no hero, friend or foe, would heed 
the warning in her words of future doom.
In Reverie my curse is surely worse.
For every word I utter, whether jest
or greetings lightly made is turned
to omen or portentous proof of something
horrid yet to come. Though I protest
and I confess my farsight in me's lacking,
the warriors of the watch are drawn to fear
my reputation as a sorcerer and seer.

For in this hour where witches roam and Nightmares
write anew our fabled tales. I say 
"hello" and off they go headlong into 
battle. I say "goodnight, what starry night"
and sleepless hours await them hatching plans.
I find myself running hither to the fray
a-casting spells to save the day my careless 
words had set so rapidly afoot.
And so my Vivian, I beg for mercy
not for me but those who need truesight.

### The witch of nightmares holds all keys

I dreamt a dream last night, a songe sent by
my devil father speaking with his guile.
He helped me see quite woefully that you
were not the sorceress who'd opened up 
my cage, nor laid the curse to steal the powers
gifted by my spawner. A wailful witch 
named Baba Yaga was she alone to blame. 
"Woe on her, her doom is nigh," according
to my father. Omen spoken, words 
in demon tongue, a prophecy from hell.

Blast this witch of icy climes from o'er 
the Volga vales. I take the fight to Baba!
Her nightmare minions hail from nowhere known
on earth, nor hell, nor Otherworldly realm.
My fellow Raven keepers are too human 
to avail. In fact I've grown too fond of them 
to let them risk their lives in battle with this 
cauldron wielding witch from sleepless hell.
Since every word I speak to them they turn
to prophecy, I've told them I must fight
this fight alone, "'Tis written in the stars."
And so my love farewell to thee, should Baba
Yaga triumph, task to lock this witch 
away and save what's left of Reverie.

### Heroes born to destinies unknown

Sweet Vivian I have returned a victor
o'er the witch. Our Baba Yaga fought
with savage fury, equal to my spells.
I would have died had not the Watch
appeared in greater numbers than her minions
could repel. She finally fell to druid 
magic, mixed with snow-queen ice, and dragon
fire, a piper's song, a mermaid's chant,
and wolfen warrior's thrashings. Flax-haired maiden 
boy in tow, with Romeo and lovely 
Juliet. Geppetto backed Carmilla as
Aladdin with Wukong closed out our ranks.

My prophecy of single battle, I 
against the witch, they'd thrown aside and leapt
to fray, their superstitions swept aside. 
'twas then I saw what makes a man or woman
brave. To face one's death, a certainty,
defying even fate for greater good.
If prophecy has any worth it cannot
be in champion's song. For future sight 
it sheds no light, but steals the hero's heart.
I must ponder this predicament
until I've learned the lesson meant for me.

### What future can we have when all is writ

At last I now can see just why you locked
your mentor fast in stone. Our fleshly commerce
set aside, our harps, our singing through the night.
There were no future choices made beyond 
the verse your lover voiced in parchment and 
on stone. How ever could you learn from follies
of your own device, if bye and bye 
the words I'd woven traced your every step.

I pen this final missive, dear, written 
without malice. Hopeful how your heart
will read it, for my future sight has went
and gone. I've come to love these days of question: 
mornings bear a sentiment of hope, 'til night
comes all too quickly, sleep a promise of
another day. Finally I have cast 
aside my demon gifts, more truly now 
a human son I've finally become.
Would you, my Vivian, return to love 
a man without a vision for the future? 
Nothing more have I to give, all powers 
you have mastered, all my secrets you've 
revealed. There's nothing left in me but love 
for thee, my harp and legendary wit.
